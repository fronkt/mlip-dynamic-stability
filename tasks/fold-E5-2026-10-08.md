The one-sentence result: fine-tuning toward PBE fixes the surface along the held-out coordinates
but does not fix a single pre-registered screen call. An unregistered diagnostic (Section 5) traces
that to the lattice: fine-tuned without stresses, both models relax BaTiO3 and KNbO3 0.3-0.9 %
smaller (to within 0.4 % of the PBE vc-relax lattice), and at that lattice the wells are shallow
enough to stay stable at 300 K. At the base model's lattice, where all held-out PBE (and the paper's
Section 3.2 PBE check) was computed, 11 of 12 fine-tuned replicate x system runs call 300 K
correctly. This makes the paper's "the BaTiO3/KNbO3 mis-calls disappear on PBE" a statement about
PBE at the MLIP's lattice, not at PBE's own. E2 pbe-lattice phases B/C (PBE phonons and wells at the
PBE lattice) are now the decisive missing check.

Three things in the record need a deviation entry before anything is written into the paper
(Section 2): MACE ran 6 epochs, not the registered 30 (D2 not executed); the 403 extra-mode
held-out points were not in the evaluation; the P1 per-replicate rule lives in code, not in the
registration text.

So: on the ferroelectrics fine-tuning makes the MLIP reproduce PBE's wells and PBE's calls on PBE's
coordinates; on CsSnBr3 it moves the MLIP AWAY from PBE (MACE agreement 31 to 23-25 of 32; depth
ratio 0.92 to 1.5-1.7). Plausible causes, not separated here: the CsSnBr3 training sets are the
ones most altered by D1 (41 % and 10 % acceptance at 600 K), its per-system energy offset leaves a
16.7 (MACE) and 36.6 (CHGNet) meV/atom residual spread, and E2 (5ac1a79) found the CsSnBr3 PBE well
depth itself not k-point converged. The 300 K unit calls on BaTiO3 and KNbO3, re-solved on these
paths, are correct (unstable) in every fine-tuned replicate of both models; the base models call
them stable. That is the fixed-coordinate counterpart of P2, and it is the opposite of what the
production pipeline returns.

## 5. Exploratory (NOT pre-registered): why P1 succeeds and P2 fails

### 5.1 Observation

### 5.3 Reading, and its limits

- The P2 refutation is a lattice effect: 0/12 fine-tuned runs correct at their own lattice, 11/12 at
  the base lattice (MACE 6/6, CHGNet 5/6; CHGNet seed 0 KNbO3 stays stable at both).
- The fine-tuned lattices are an extrapolation: every training configuration was at the base
  lattice and no stresses were trained, so the contraction is not a learned PBE equation of state,
  even though it lands near the PBE lattice. Whether PBE at its own lattice calls BaTiO3 and KNbO3
  unstable at 300 K is untested; the fine-tuned models predict it would not, at least within this
  single-mode screen. E2 pbe-lattice phases B/C answer this directly.
- Consequence for the paper: Table S19 already says PBE was evaluated at each MLIP's lattice. The
  sentences in the Abstract, Section 3.2/4 and Conclusions that the BaTiO3/KNbO3 mis-calls
  "disappear on PBE" need that qualifier, and Section 4's "a fine-tune that deepened the softened
  ferroelectric wells toward PBE would plausibly remove about half the shared count" is now
  contradicted by a direct test of the production pipeline.
- P3 is affected too: it ran at the fine-tuned model's own lattice (4.0162 A), where its well is
  near base depth, so it does not test "a surface with PBE-deep wells".
- If any number from 5.2 goes into the paper or response, commit the two scripts (e.g.
  `scripts/finetune_lattice_diag.py`) and their JSON output under `results/revision/finetune/`, and
  label them as post hoc everywhere.

## 6. What the trial does NOT show

1. It does not show that fine-tuning cannot fix finite-temperature screening. Two models, three
   systems, 120 configurations at a single lattice, no stresses, and for MACE 6 epochs instead of
   the registered 30. A fine-tune with stress labels or multi-lattice data could behave differently.
2. It does not show that PBE at its own lattice calls BaTiO3/KNbO3 unstable at 300 K, nor that it
   does not. The diagnostic is a model extrapolation.
3. It does not test experiment: fine-tuning moves the model toward PBE, and on CsSnBr3 PBE is itself
   wrong (E2: PBEsol fixes 8 of the 9 CsSnBr3 PBE errors) and not k-converged in well depth. The
   CsSnBr3 "persist" verdicts test the reference functional, not the screen or the MLIP.
4. It does not test generality of forgetting: S2 is six harmonic controls; CHGNet's four unresolved
   cells include two base-model errors partly repaired.
5. P1 rests on 2 (MACE) and 3 (CHGNet) deciding paths and, for MACE, two systems; the registered
   extra-mode held-out points were not evaluated.
6. It does not support any significance statement (three systems; registration says descriptive).
7. P3 is one seed, one unit, at the contracted lattice.
8. It says nothing about the other three architectures, about phonon-data fine-tunes (ref 41), or
   about the model-uncertainty question of R2.2.

### 8(a) Manuscript subsection (405 words), proposed as §3.6 after §3.5

**3.6 Fine-tuning toward PBE: a pre-registered trial**

To test whether the mis-calls of §3.2 and §3.3 survive fine-tuning, MACE-MP-0 and CHGNet were
fine-tuned toward PBE under a protocol registered before any data were computed (ESI §S6). Each
model was fine-tuned three times (seeds 0, 1 and 2) on PBE energies and forces of 120
phonon-rattled 2×2×2 supercells of BaTiO₃, KNbO₃ and CsSnBr₃ (108 for training, 12 for
validation), with each tool's documented small-data recipe. The test set was the PBE data along the
screen's own soft-mode coordinates (§2.6); no training configuration lies within 0.08 Å RMS of it.
A call counts as changed only if all three replicates agree.

Fine-tuning recovered the PBE surface along those coordinates. In the well region the MACE-MP-0
force error fell from 0.032 to 0.004–0.012 eV Å⁻¹ on BaTiO₃ and from 0.090 to 0.006–0.014 eV Å⁻¹ on
KNbO₃, and the median ratio of fine-tuned to PBE well depth on the deciding paths rose from 0.45 to
1.00, as predicted. For CHGNet the pooled ratio was 1.04, but one replicate overshot (1.33), so
that prediction is unresolved. Re-solved on these fixed paths, the 300 K calls on BaTiO₃ and KNbO₃
are correct in every replicate of both models.

The full screen did not follow. Re-run from relaxation onward with each fine-tuned model, it still
called BaTiO₃ and KNbO₃ stable at 300 K in all three replicates of both models, so the prediction
that fine-tuning corrects these mis-calls is refuted in all four cells. The KNbO₃ 600 K and
CsSnBr₃ 300 K mis-calls persisted, as predicted. MACE-MP-0 also lost a correct call, CsSnBr₃ at
900 K, in all three replicates. Converged SSCHA on fine-tuned MACE-MP-0 still called BaTiO₃ stable
at 100 K (1.74 THz), as predicted from the locality of its criterion (§3.3). MACE-MP-0 kept all six
control calls; CHGNet left four of six unresolved.

A check made after the results, and not registered, places the gap in the lattice. Trained without
stresses, the fine-tuned models relax BaTiO₃ and KNbO₃ 0.3 to 0.9% smaller than the base models,
to within 0.4% of the PBE lattice. At that lattice none of the 12 replicate and system pairs calls
300 K unstable; at the base model's lattice, where the PBE reference was computed, 11 do. The 300 K
corrections of §3.2 therefore hold at each MLIP's lattice and are untested at PBE's own. MACE-MP-0
was trained for 6 epochs rather than the registered 30, an execution error recorded in ESI §S6.

              for s in reps),
          "[E5] S1 MACE-MP-0: held-out CsSnBr3 energy error RISES (0.15 -> 0.60-0.85 meV/atom) after fine-tuning")
    with open(ft + "dataset/mace_mp0/dataset.json", encoding="utf-8") as fh:

          and all(e["guard"]["n_violations"] == 0 and e["guard"]["min_nearest_A"] > 0.05
                  for e in man["entries"].values())
          and min(e["guard"]["min_nearest_A"] for e in man["entries"].values()) > 0.079,
          "[E5] 108/12 train/valid split; leakage guard: 0 configurations within 0.05 A of a held-out "
