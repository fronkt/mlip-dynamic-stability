## D1 — SSCHA discarded the instability it was testing for  [VERIFIED]

`finite_t.py`, `compute_finite_t_sscha` (pre-fix):

```python
w = np.sort(np.asarray(w))
nonac = w[3:] if w.size > 3 else w      # "drop the 3 acoustic zero modes"
```

cellconstructor returns imaginary modes as **negative**. Sorting ascending therefore puts the *soft
mode* first, not the acoustic zeros, so `w[3:]` deletes the instability and keeps the exact zeros.
The ledger convicts the code, because `ft_lowest6_thz` stores what was thrown away:

| system / model / T | `ft_lowest6_thz` | recorded min | call |
|---|---|---|---|
| PbTiO₃ / SevenNet-0 / 100 K | `[-0.215, -0.215, -0.215, -0.0, -0.0, 0.0]` | −3.4e−7 | stable |
| PbTiO₃ / MACE-MP-0 / 100 K | `[-0.393, -0.393, -0.393, -0.0, -0.0, -0.0]` | −2.5e−7 | stable |
| SrTiO₃ / MACE-MP-0 / 100 K | `[-0.962, -0.962, -0.962, 0.0, 0.0, 0.0]` | +1.1e−7 | stable |

In each case the triply degenerate soft mode is more negative than the acoustic zeros. The
fingerprint of a corrupted row is a reported minimum of order 10⁻⁷ THz.

**Damage: 13 of 208 SSCHA rows, every one of them a manufactured false-stable.**

Recomputed with acoustic modes identified by `argsort(|ω|)[:3]`:

| quantity | published | corrected |
|---|---|---|
| FE-perovskite SSCHA recall, T ≤ 300 K (n=30) | 0.23 | **0.30** |
| fluorite SSCHA recall, all T (n=40) | 0.17 | **0.23** |
| soft-mode screen recall, same FE set | 0.77 | 0.77 (unaffected) |
| **bcc rows changed (n=75)** | — | **0** |

**The §3.3 headline survives.** 21 of 30 FE units are still genuinely false-stable, the bcc
gold-standard family is untouched in all 75 rows, and the ρ = 0.78 method agreement is unaffected.
The bug inflated the effect; it did not create it. Every affected number must still be restated.

Fixed in `87a9183`.

## D2 — the Γ mask deleted the ferroelectric soft mode  [VERIFIED]

`finite_t.py`, `_softest_mesh_mode` (pre-fix):

```python
fr[qi, np.argsort(fr[qi])[:3]] = np.inf     # "mask the 3 acoustic branches at Gamma"
```

Same inversion. At Γ in cubic BaTiO₃ the three *lowest* branches are the T1u ferroelectric triplet
near −6.9 THz; the acoustic branches sit at ~0. The mask therefore removed the ferroelectric mode
and kept the translations, so **Γ could never win the q-search** — and no perovskite unit in the
grid ever selected it. The docstring's claim that "a real zone-centre FE soft mode is optical and
stays found" is exactly backwards.

Consequences for the manuscript:
- §3.5's rebuttal — *"it occurs for the Γ ferroelectric mode of BaTiO₃, which is present in every
  cell including the 2×2×2 used"* — is **false**; that mode was never the one screened.
- Every FE-perovskite well depth, `m_eff`, `T*` and order parameter describes an off-Γ mode.
- Mitigating (and worth stating rather than accidentally relying on): BaTiO₃'s FE branch is
  quasi-flat along ⟨100⟩, so an off-Γ q on that branch captures similar physics. That has to be an
  argument in the text, not an accident in the code.

Fixed in `87a9183`.

## D3 — the deposited code cannot reproduce the deposited ledger  [VERIFIED]

**220 of 340 non-bcc softmode rows carry a modulation dimension > 2.** From a 2×2×2
force-constant supercell the commensurate q are {0, ½}³, so `limit_denominator(2)` can only ever
return 1 or 2. Denominator 6 is arithmetically impossible. `cli.py` gives 6×6×6 to `bcc-metal`
only, yet `control` rows are 100% dim > 2 and `perovskite` rows 62%.

Root cause, and it is fully mechanical:

- `89212ff` (Jun 21) implemented an **"exact D6 q-search"** — denominator-6 q on 2×2×2 FCs.
- `e592e86` (Jun 22) replaced it: *"FC-commensurate q-search only (fix unsound interpolation)"*,
  the author's own note in `todo.md` naming **"den-6-on-2x2x2 was interpolating spurious flat-well
  modes"**.
- But `settings` passed to `unit_hash` was only `{"supercell": [...]}`. Changing the *algorithm*
  did not change the *hash*, so `ledger.has_unit` returned True and **every stale unit was skipped
  as "already present."** The fix landed in the code; the data was never regenerated.

`e592e86` is an ancestor of all three ledger commits, which is why this survived to the deposit.

**So the entire primary-screen layer — all 400 softmode rows, hence §3.2, §3.4, §3.5 and the screen
side of §3.3 — was computed by a q-search the author identified as unsound and fixed.** This is the
one that ends the paper in review if a referee runs the code, and it is also the one that
invalidates §2.4's central *soundness* argument.

Fixed in `87a9183` by folding `METHOD_VERSION` into the unit hash and the E(Q) cache key, so an
algorithm change can never again be skipped as already-present. **The softmode grid must be re-run.**

## D4 — dead code shipped as method  [VERIFIED]

`_solve_1d` (`finite_t.py:564`), the exact 1D quantum solver, is **never called** — nothing
references it. `compute_finite_t_softmode`'s docstring nevertheless describes the method as
"exact 1D quantum thermal density → stability from the potential-of-mean-force curvature," which is
`_solve_1d`'s algorithm, not the `_solve_scha` variational one actually used. Either promote it to
a reported cross-check or delete it; do not ship both.

## Novelty: the framing has to change  [REPORTED, two anchors verified]

Two papers were checked directly and both resolve:

1. **Yang, Yin, Ao & Li**, *MACE Foundation Models for Lattice Dynamics: A Benchmark Study on
   Double Halide Perovskites*, arXiv:2510.18178 (21 Oct 2025) → **PCCP 28, 4459–4469 (2026)**,
   doi:10.1039/D5CP04693A. Benchmarks four MACE foundation variants for the dynamic stability of
   halide double perovskites and reports that accuracy degrades for the highly anharmonic,
   dynamically unstable cases.
2. **"Are Foundational Atomistic Models Reliable for Finite-Temperature Molecular Dynamics?"**,
   *J. Phys. Chem. C*, doi:10.1021/acs.jpcc.5c07541, online 27 Nov 2025. Uses the
   ferroelectric–paraelectric transition in **PbTiO₃** — one of our own 20 systems — to report a
   disconnect between static accuracy and dynamic reliability.

Therefore **"essentially un-benchmarked" (line ~49) and "blind spot" (title, Conclusions) are not
defensible.** What survives is narrower and still real: no one has run a **cross-family** (five
architectures, not four variants of one) finite-temperature dynamic-stability classification across
FE + AFD + halide perovskites + bcc refractory metals + cubic fluorites with a free-energy criterion
and a temperature ladder. Claim that, and only that.

Further [REPORTED], not yet human-verified, and load-bearing enough that they must be read before
citing:
- **Bianco et al., PRB 96, 014111 (2017)** reportedly states that the SCHA Φ is positive-definite
  *by construction* and derives the free-energy Hessian precisely so a fixed high-symmetry reference
  *can* detect displacive transitions. If so, "ForcePositiveDefinite erases the soft mode" is not a
  discovery but a restatement of the method's design, and the §3.3 root-cause paragraph must be
  rewritten. **Read this paper first — it is the single highest-stakes citation in the review.**
- **Monacelli, PRB 112, 014109 (2025)** reportedly states verbatim that looking for sign changes in
  the auxiliary matrix "is trivially wrong."
- H3 [REPORTED]: α-AgI is excluded by a substring filter; restoring it moves the vote-split AUC
  0.745 → 0.690 and the "5.5×" enrichment → 3.6×. Verify before the guardrail claim is restated.

## What was rejected

The review's synthesized plan proposed reframing the paper around "stability is the argmin of the
free energy over the centroid, not its curvature at the high-symmetry reference," plus a deployment
criterion built on that. An adversarial physics pass refuted it and I am **not** implementing it:
for an even V about a centred Gaussian all odd moments vanish, so Φ₃ = ⟨V'''⟩₀ = 0 and the
free-energy Hessian at Q₀ = 0 reduces to the auxiliary curvature. The centroid/curvature distinction
is then a criterion mismatch, not a physical discovery, and a criterion built on it would predict an
artefact of our own comparison.
