## Section 3a — Objectives

My work asks whether the benchmarks used to rank foundation machine-learning interatomic
potentials measure the property those models are actually deployed to predict. On a curated set of
systems it turns out they do not: 0 K harmonic phonon accuracy fails to predict finite-temperature
dynamic-stability accuracy in either direction, and MLIP-driven SSCHA at its default bubble
truncation systematically declares deep displacive instabilities stable. The Intelligent
Computation session is where the people who build, benchmark and deploy these potentials will be,
and I have three objectives in presenting there. First, to put a negative benchmarking result in
front of exactly the groups who set those benchmarks, since a critique of evaluation practice is
only useful if the people defining the practice hear it. Second, to have the result attacked
properly: the objection I most expect is that my harmonic estimator is simply noisy, and I would
rather have that pressure-tested in person by people who run these calculations daily than
discover the hole in review. Third, to learn how this community would extend the screen past the
fifteen curated systems I was able to run alone, which is the natural next step and the one where
I have the least experience to draw on.
